# Montréal — environnement géographique

`dist/montreal/environment.js` exports `addEnvironment(THREE, ctx)`. It adds a named group and returns `{group, landmarks, isWater, groundMask, landY, waterY, stats}`. The shared engine owns the registered race path, relief, route surfaces, barriers, pit lane, viewer and illustrative cars. This scenery adds no replay or fabricated telemetry.

## Geographic placement

Every major landmark is projected through `ctx.config.geography`: x east, z south, the same positive scale as the parent circuit. The island outlines, basin, lake outlines, Casino footprints, Biosphère footprint and bridge alignment come from the retained OpenStreetMap extract. They are not rotated to suit a printed map. The Biosphère is on **Île Sainte-Hélène**, west/northwest of the hairpin across the river channel; it is not placed in the circuit's infield. The Casino's France and Québec pavilions retain separate footprints on **Île Notre-Dame**. The bridge remains on its actual distant northern alignment.

The horizon water disk has an 18 km radius, below the island ground. At the current registered circuit's lowest road level, land is about y=1.45 m and water y=0.25 m. The shared far-ground plane must remain below the water. `isWater(x,z)` masks the detailed shared terrain at the river, Olympic basin and internal lakes; `groundMask` provides the complementary land predicate. All reconstructed racing samples lie on the island. The distant South Shore is a coarse geographic approximation and should not be used as a map; Notre-Dame and Sainte-Hélène use the retained detailed outlines.

## Recognizable geometry

- Notre-Dame and Sainte-Hélène shorelines, river channel, retaining banks and the long **1976 Olympic basin**, with eight rowing/canoe lanes and instanced buoy lines. Basin dimensions and lane context follow Parc Jean-Drapeau's technical description; the exact water plan follows OSM.
- The **Biosphère** is an open triangular steel lattice, with 1,594 instanced members, a roughly 76 m diameter / 62 m high envelope, museum floor volumes and a circular plaza. It deliberately has no opaque glass sphere. The structural centre follows the OSM footprint; the official park page's GPS coordinate is the public arrival point nearby. The frequency, internal floors and member sizes are procedural approximations, not a replica of the engineering drawings.
- The **Casino** has two geographically distinct buildings: white horizontal bands, layered floor plates and glazed façades for the France pavilion; a separate glazed Québec pavilion. White supports extend into the reflecting-basin foundation level. Heights, façade details and roof profiles are approximate; the real footprints and architectural identity are retained.
- **11 open grandstand volumes** represent the start/finish, Senna, T6–7, T8–9 and hairpin viewing zones, including 15/21/24 around the hairpin. Each has individual seating rows, steel support legs meeting the terrain, railings and identifiers. Positions follow nearby registered road frames, and some footprints are reduced to preserve route clearance and island shoreline. Grandstand 34 is omitted because the reconstructed narrow infield cannot hold it with the required safety reserve. These are scenery, not a seating/capacity plan or a ticketing guide. The current GP sales page now advertises 2027, so its zone descriptions are used alongside the retained 2026 FIA/visitor documents rather than as a claim that all 2027 inventory existed in 2026.
- The **Wall of Champions** follows the outside of the T14 exit, on the driver's right, with 23 painted concrete modules and readable original text. It is a scenery wall beyond the common safety boundary, not an added collision object at the apex. Advertising appearance is approximate.
- The distant green **Jacques-Cartier cantilever bridge** has a 23.1 m deck, truss webs, top/lower chords, parapets and 32 support volumes along the real road alignment. The deck width follows the bridge operator; 49 m nominal deck height above water, truss profile and support count/locations are visual approximations. This is a landmark silhouette, not a load-bearing or navigation model.
- **1,400 deciduous park trees**, with instanced trunks and three batched crown palettes, avoid the water, circuit, pit lane, grandstands and major buildings. No external image textures or building assets are used.

Solids use at least a **4 m reserve beyond the nominal 5.5 m road half-width**, also tested against the pit lane through the shared `drivingDistance`. Grandstand footprints are sampled across their area, tested against water and checked for overlap with one another. Trunks and the full canopy radius receive additional clearance. Building foundations use `ctx.groundHeightAt`, which follows the visible terrain; the Casino's basin supports extend below the water level.

## Verification

The module imports and constructs successfully with the registered Montréal configuration and headless Three.js. A temporary QA harness verifies no reconstructed racing sample lies in water, landmark presence/counts, grandstand land/footprint clearance and every solid mesh's world-space vertices, including all instances, against the driving corridors. The first audit covered **1,177,477 solid vertices** and found at least **4.41 m nominal road-edge clearance**.

The final pass uses the actual shared `createCircuitWorld`, its real pit lane and visible-terrain sampler: **11 grandstands, 1,400 trees, 1,594 lattice members, 32 bridge supports and 23 wall modules** construct successfully; **zero road samples are in water**. Conservative bounding-volume checks clear the distant objects, then **13,492 near-corridor solid vertices** pass the exact sampler with the same minimum **4.41 m reserve**. Shared static batching reduces 33 source meshes into 9 batches, saving 24 draw calls; the tested solid landmark groups contain 86 draw meshes. Browser appearance and final integrated controls remain part of the parent's visual QA.
